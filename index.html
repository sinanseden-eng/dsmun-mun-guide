🏛️ THIMUN Language Guide

Master the Art of Parliamentary Discourse

🎯 Golden Rule

Always speak in third person! Never say "I think..." → say "The delegate of [Country] believes..."

⭐ Mastering Third Person ▼
Situation❌ Don't Say✅ Say This
Opinion"I think...""The delegate believes..."
Motion"I motion to...""The delegate motions..."
Agreement"We agree with...""The delegation concurs with..."
Question"I want to ask...""The delegate wishes to pose a question..."

🙋‍♀️ Points & Motions

1️⃣ Point of Information ▼

Ask a question to the speaker. Cannot interrupt a speech. The speaker must first yield to Points of Information.

How to use: "The delegate of [Country] would like to raise a Point of Information to the speaker."

2️⃣ Point of Order ▼

Used when there is a procedural error. Cannot interrupt a speech.

How to use: "Point of Order, the delegate believes the proper procedure has not been followed regarding..."

The Chair will respond: "Your point is well taken" or "Your point is not well taken."

3️⃣ Point of Personal Privilege ▼

Used for audibility issues only. This is the ONLY point that can interrupt a speech — and only if the delegate cannot hear the speaker.

How to use: "Point of Personal Privilege — the delegate cannot hear the speaker."

4️⃣ Motion to Close Debate ▼

Ends debate and moves to voting. Requires a vote by the House.

How to use: "The delegate motions to close debate on the resolution."

5️⃣ Motion to Extend Debate ▼

Requests more debate time. Requires a vote.

How to use: "The delegate motions to extend debate time by 5 minutes for and 5 minutes against."

💡 THIMUN Pro Tips

  • Jacket On: A delegate must have their jacket/blazer on anytime they stand to speak.
  • Respect the Chair: The Chair's rulings are final.
  • Stand Up: Always stand when speaking.
  • Be Diplomatic: Attack policies, not delegates.
  • One Thing at a Time: One resolution and one amendment at a time.
  • No Interruptions: Speakers cannot be interrupted except for audibility.
  • Voting is Sacred: No interruptions once voting begins except Point of Order related to the vote.

📜 Resolution Writing Guide

THIMUN Style — From Header to Final Period

What is a Resolution?

A resolution is a single, long sentence that proposes a solution to a problem. It is split into two main parts:

  • Preambulatory Clauses (The "WHY"): Explains background and context.
  • Operative Clauses (The "HOW"): The action plan.

Part 1: The Header

This is the "address" of your resolution. It must be 100% correct.

FORUM: General Assembly First Committee
QUESTION OF: The role of diamonds in fueling conflict...
SUBMITTED BY: Saint Vincent and the Grenadines
CO-SUBMITTERS: Argentina, Australia, Canada, France...
The Header must be perfect. FORUM and QUESTION OF must match the official agenda exactly. CO-SUBMITTERS must be listed in alphabetical order.

Part 2: Preamble (The "WHY")

This is the introduction. Remember:

  • Start with the full committee name in ALL CAPS (e.g., THE DISARMAMENT COMMISSION,)
  • Clause starters are italicized.
  • Every clause ends with a comma (,).

Common preamble starters:

Aware of
Bearing in mind
Deeply concerned
Declaring
Emphasizing
Guided by
Having examined
Keeping in mind
Noting with regret
Reaffirming
Recalling
Recognizing
Full Example Preamble ▼
Aware of that 4% of diamonds produced are classified as "conflict or blood diamonds",

Recognizing the Kimberley Process Certification Scheme (KPCS) as an internationally recognized certification system,

Further recognizing that the KPCS is lacking a comprehensive system for monitoring where diamonds are mined, how they are mined and how proceeds are used,

Part 3: Operative (The "HOW")

Remember:

  • Clauses are numbered (1, 2, 3...).
  • Clause starters are underlined.
  • Every clause ends with a semicolon (;), except the last which ends with a period (.)

Common operative starters:

Approves
Asks
Authorizes
Calls for
Urges
Requests
Encourages
Recommends
Operative Example & Sub-clauses ▼
1. Calls for all relevant Member States to sign and ratify the KPCS to promote cooperation and awareness of trade;

2. Further calls for all relevant nations which are withholding signature to request funds from IGOs such as IMF and World Bank;

3. Asks for the creation of the Collation for Stopping Conflict Diamonds (CSCD), which would work with Member States by:
a. monitoring who is buying and selling the diamonds
b. ensuring the methods are legal according to KPCS
c. sending submitted proof of diamond proceeds
d. sending an observer team in the first year following ratification;

Do's and Don'ts

Resolution Do's and Don'ts ▼

✅ Do...

  • Write the entire resolution as one sentence.
  • End preambulatory clauses with a comma.
  • End operative clauses with a semicolon; last clause with a period.
  • List co-submitters alphabetically.

❌ Don't...

  • Use personal pronouns (I, we, our).
  • Repeat clause starters without 'Further'.
  • Have lists with a single item (a. requires b.).

🧑‍⚖️ Chair Simulator

Practice Chair Dialogue with Audio

Opening the Session

"The House will come to order."

Used to start the session or regain control of the room.

"Roll call. When your country is called, please respond with 'Present' or 'Present and voting.'"

Begin by taking attendance of all delegations.

Introducing a Resolution

"The chair calls upon the delegate of [Country] to read the resolution to the House."
"The House has heard the resolution. Is there a second?"

Requires at least one other delegation to support bringing it to debate.

Managing Debate

"The Chair sets debate time at 10 minutes for and 10 minutes against the resolution."
"The floor is now open for debate. Are there any delegates wishing to speak for the resolution?"
"Your point is well taken." / "Your point is not well taken."

Formal accept/reject of a Point of Order.

"The chair proposes an extension of debate time by 5 minutes for and 5 minutes against."

Voting Procedures

"The debate is now closed. We will move into voting procedures. All points are out of order."

Critical announcement — all note-passing and discussion is now prohibited.

"Will all those in favour of the resolution please raise their placards."
"Will all those against the resolution please raise their placards."
"Will all those abstaining please raise their placards."
"With X votes in favour, Y votes against, and Z abstaining, the resolution has passed/failed."
"Clapping is (not) in order."

Used to maintain decorum after a vote result.

🗣️ Speech Studio

Structure & Practice Persuasive Speeches

How to Structure a Speech (60–90 Seconds)

1. Opening (10 sec): State your position clearly.
"The delegation of [Country] rises in strong support/opposition..."
2. Key Points (40–60 sec): Give 2–3 strong arguments.
Use facts, statistics, or references to treaties and UN resolutions.
3. Closing (10 sec): Reinforce your position.
"For these reasons, the delegation urges the House to vote in favour/against."

Example Speeches

Topic: Global Climate Adaptation Fund

✅ Speech FOR (Bangladesh)

"The delegation of Bangladesh rises in strong support. This fund addresses a fundamental injustice. The nations least responsible for climate change are the ones suffering the most. This is not charity — it is climate justice."

❌ Speech AGAINST (Singapore)

"Singapore must respectfully oppose. The commitment is economically unrealistic. Binding financial obligations without clear accountability mechanisms will lead to misallocation. This formula is neither fair nor effective."

Topic: Digital Privacy & Data Protection

✅ Speech FOR (Germany)

"Germany strongly supports this essential resolution. Citizens' data is being harvested at an unprecedented scale without consent. Privacy and progress are not mutually exclusive."

❌ Speech AGAINST (USA)

"The United States firmly opposes this. Overly strict data regulations will stifle innovation and harm the global digital economy. This creates dangerous censorship potential."

Topic: Universal Access to Quality Education

✅ Speech FOR (Nigeria)

"Nigeria stands in firm support. Over 20 million children in our country alone lack access to basic education. Education is not a privilege — it is a fundamental human right."

❌ Speech AGAINST (Japan)

"Japan respectfully questions the feasibility. The resolution lacks a concrete funding mechanism. Good intentions without practical implementation help no one."

Topic: Regulation of Artificial Intelligence

✅ Speech FOR (France)

"France wholeheartedly supports regulation. AI systems are making life-altering decisions without transparency or accountability. We must govern AI before AI governs us."

❌ Speech AGAINST (South Korea)

"South Korea respectfully opposes. Premature regulation risks killing innovation. The cure should not be worse than the disease."

Topic: Equitable Vaccine Distribution

✅ Speech FOR (India)

"India rises in strong support. No nation is safe until every nation is safe. Hoarding vaccines prolongs the pandemic for everyone. Vaccine equity is not generosity — it is strategy."

❌ Speech AGAINST (UK)

"The United Kingdom respectfully disagrees with the proposed mechanism. Compulsory sharing undermines intellectual property rights. Innovation must be rewarded, not punished."

Topic: Phasing Out Fossil Fuel Subsidies

✅ Speech FOR (Sweden)

"Sweden strongly supports this resolution. Fossil fuel subsidies amount to $5.9 trillion annually — that is staggering. We are literally paying to destroy our own planet."

❌ Speech AGAINST (Saudi Arabia)

"Saudi Arabia firmly opposes. An abrupt phase-out would devastate developing economies. Energy transition must be gradual, not catastrophic."
